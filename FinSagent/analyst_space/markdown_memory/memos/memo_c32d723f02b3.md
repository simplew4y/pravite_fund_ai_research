## 竞争格局

NVIDIA operates in a fiercely competitive semiconductor and accelerated computing market, with key rivals including Advanced Micro Devices (AMD), Intel Corporation (INTC), and increasingly, large cloud and tech firms like Meta Platforms (META) and Amazon (not explicitly named but implied through in-house development trends) [3], [4]. AMD remains NVIDIA’s most direct competitor in discrete GPUs and data center AI accelerators, leveraging its CDNA and RDNA architectures to challenge NVIDIA’s dominance in both gaming and AI workloads. Intel, historically focused on CPUs, has re-entered the discrete GPU market with its Arc lineup and is investing heavily in AI chips, including its Gaudi accelerators, signaling intensified rivalry in high-performance computing [3]. Additionally, companies such as Broadcom and Qualcomm are expanding into AI inference and edge computing with custom SoCs, further fragmenting the competitive landscape [2].

Market share dynamics are shifting rapidly as demand for AI accelerators surges. While NVIDIA currently commands a dominant position in AI training—estimated by third parties to exceed 80%—this leadership is under threat from well-resourced competitors [4]. Some rivals, notably Intel and AMD, operate their own fabrication facilities, granting them potential supply chain advantages during periods of constrained foundry capacity—a vulnerability for NVIDIA, which relies entirely on TSMC and other external foundries [4]. Moreover, hyperscalers like Meta and Google are developing in-house AI chips (e.g., TPUs), reducing reliance on third-party GPUs and potentially limiting NVIDIA’s addressable market over time [4].

NVIDIA’s primary competitive advantages lie in its full-stack approach: superior hardware performance, a robust software ecosystem (including CUDA, which has become a de facto industry standard), and strong developer mindshare [1], [2]. However, its disadvantages include dependency on external manufacturing and exposure to pricing pressure from lower-cost alternatives [4]. Competitors with larger balance sheets—such as Intel and IBM—may also outspend NVIDIA in R&D or subsidize hardware to gain design wins [4].
