However, regulatory scrutiny is intensifying due to its AI market dominance, with inquiries from the EU, UK, China, and others regarding GPU sales, supply allocation, and foundation model partnerships—posing potential compliance and operational risks [6].

## 竞争格局

NVIDIA operates in a highly dynamic and intensifying competitive landscape, primarily facing off against established semiconductor giants like Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) and Intel Corporation (INTC), both of which are aggressively expanding into GPUs, CPUs, DPUs, and AI-accelerated computing [3]. AMD remains NVIDIA’s most direct rival in discrete GPUs for gaming and data centers, while Intel has recently entered the high-performance GPU market with its own offerings, signaling a strategic shift that could pressure NVIDIA’s dominance [3]. Beyond traditional chipmakers, competition also stems from hyperscalers such as Meta Platforms (META) and others who possess in-house silicon capabilities and may develop custom AI accelerators to reduce reliance on third-party vendors like NVIDIA [4]. Additionally, companies providing high-performance interconnect technologies—based on InfiniBand, Ethernet, or proprietary protocols—represent another layer of competitive threat in AI and HPC infrastructure [2].

Market share dynamics reflect NVIDIA’s current leadership in AI accelerators and data center GPUs, but this position is increasingly contested. While specific market share percentages are not provided in the evidence, NVIDIA acknowledges that competitors with greater financial, manufacturing, and distribution resources—such as Intel and AMD—may more effectively adapt to technological shifts or customer demands [1][4]. Furthermore, some rivals operate their own fabrication facilities (e.g., Intel), granting them potential supply chain advantages during periods of foundry constraint, whereas NVIDIA relies on third-party foundries like TSMC [4].
