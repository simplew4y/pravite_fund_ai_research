## 竞争格局

NVIDIA faces intensifying competition in the AI and data center semiconductor market, particularly as U.S. export controls have effectively foreclosed its ability to serve China’s data center market with its H20 chip [1]. This exclusion has created a strategic opening for domestic Chinese competitors—such as Huawei, Biren, and Moore Threads—to capture significant market share within China and build developer ecosystems that could challenge NVIDIA globally over time [1]. Beyond China, NVIDIA contends with established rivals like AMD and Intel, which offer alternative GPU and AI accelerator solutions, though neither currently matches NVIDIA’s full-stack software-hardware integration or ecosystem maturity [2]. Meta Platforms’ inclusion in NVIDIA’s peer group underscores growing competition not just in silicon, but also in attracting top-tier AI and engineering talent—a critical input in maintaining technological leadership [3].

Market share dynamics are shifting due to geopolitical constraints and product availability. NVIDIA reported zero H20 sales to China-based customers in Q2 FY2026 [6], following a $4.5 billion inventory and purchase obligation charge in Q1 FY2026 tied to diminished H20 demand under new U.S. export licensing requirements [1]. While NVIDIA managed to sell $650 million of H20 units outside China in Q2 FY2026—triggering an $180 million release of prior inventory provisions [6]—its inability to serve what was once a major growth market cedes long-term opportunity to rivals. Concurrently, NVIDIA’s total inventories surged to $14.96 billion by July 2025, up from $10.08 billion in January 2025, reflecting aggressive Blackwell ramp-up and lingering H20 stock [10]. Although Blackwell demand is described as “extraordinary” [6], the company incurred significant costs from low-yielding Blackwell materials in prior periods, impacting gross margins [5].

NVIDIA’s core competitive advantages remain its CUDA software ecosystem, full-stack AI platform (including NVLink and networking), and first-mover dominance in generative AI infrastructure [6]. However, disadvantages include heavy exposure to U.S.-China trade policy volatility and high R&D intensity—R&D expenses rose 68% year-over-year in Q2 FY2026, driven by compute infrastructure and new product development [5]. Competitors with greater manufacturing or financial flexibility may adapt more quickly to regulatory shifts or price-sensitive markets [2]. Industry trends such as the rise of sovereign AI initiatives, especially in China and Europe, and increasing customer demand for diversified supply chains are accelerating the emergence of alternative architectures and regional champions, further fragmenting the competitive landscape [1].
